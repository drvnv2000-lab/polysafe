The rule

What the model is allowed to see

Every prediction is made at hour 24. Anything recorded after that line is never used as a feature, and kidney injury only counts if it starts after it. Here is one stay, played forward.

An illustrative stay drawn for this page, not a real patient. On the real tables, tests enforce the same rule.

The twist

How drugs are counted changes the answer

eICU stores medication orders, and 40.6% of them have no drug name. Brand names and code-only orders are invisible to a text search, so the same data gives different answers depending on how a drug is counted.

    Usefulness

    Would it change a decision?

    Net benefit counts correctly flagged patients, minus a penalty for false alarms that grows with the risk you would act on. Move the threshold to compare the two models with flagging everyone.

    Net benefit per 100 patients from pooled cross-validated predictions. The two models stay close at every threshold: the AUROC gain does not become a clear decision benefit.

    Associations, not causes

    Which drugs travel with kidney injury?

    Adjusted odds ratios from a propensity model, exploratory. Ondansetron is a negative control: a drug with no expected link.

    Residual confounding, including confounding by indication, remains a plausible explanation for every row.

    Honest limits

    What this does not show

    • That drugs add information beyond what a flexible clinical model already captures: LightGBM gained nothing.
    • That the model is clinically useful. No individual patient's risk is shown anywhere (78 events).
    • That the result transfers to hospitals without medication records, or to other ICUs.
    • Anything causal about these drugs.
